= Interaction model
:toc:
:toc-placement: preamble
:status: DRAFT -- design, not implementation

The behaviour, stated precisely enough to build and to test. Numbers are
defaults, not law; every one of them is a setting.

== 1. The idea in one line

A floating window that treats *being in the way* as the primary signal, and
resolves it without asking you to.

Every existing picture-in-picture implementation makes the person do collision
avoidance: notice the obstruction, grab the window, move it, remember where the
thing was, move it again. This reverses that. The window is responsible for not
obstructing, and you are responsible only for saying what you want to see.

== 2. States

[cols="1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| State | Entered by | What it looks like

| `docked`
| default
| The float, at its resting geometry, inside the work area

| `chipped`
| pointer over the float, or hotkey
| A tab of at most 48x48 px at the worked corner; restores after the delay or on
  click

| `flung`
| flick, or hotkey + arrow
| In flight, then at rest near the target edge or display; returns on click or
  on timer expiry

| `banished`
| flick off the edge, or hotkey + edge
| Virtually outside the work area; a handle of at least 4 px remains on the
  nearest edge; returns on click or on timer expiry

| `ghosted`
| policy, pointer proximity, or hotkey
| Opacity at the configured floor; still showing content; audio continues

| `clickthrough`
| automatic while `ghosted`, or explicit toggle
| Pointer events route to the page underneath; the float is unreachable until
  released

| `parked`
| explicit
| Invisible, audio-only, no pointer target; a status entry denies nothing but
  occupies nothing
|===

`chipped`, `flung` and `banished` are *displacement* states: they exist because
the float was in the way, and each has a well-defined way back. Nothing is ever
displaced without a return path, and no return path depends on the pointer
finding a moving target -- hence the persistent 4 px handle.

== 3. Triggers

|===
| Trigger | Reads | Leads to

| Pointer proximity
| Pointer position, 24 px hysteresis
| `nudge` (movement inside `docked`), or `chipped` on contact

| Caret and focus
| Active element rectangle, selection range
| `nudge` clear of the focused field, always before `chipped`

| Scroll
| Scroll events, settled after 120 ms
| During scroll: `ghosted` if configured. On settle: re-evaluate collisions

| Idle
| 30 s with no input
| Restore from `chipped`, fade from `ghosted` if it was proximity-driven

| Flick
| Pointer velocity over a drag, above 0.5 px/ms
| `flung` toward the projected edge, or the display it crosses

| Hotkey
| One key for on/off; one for each displacement; one modifier held for
  click-through release
| Mutually exclusive with pointer states; a hotkey always wins
|===

== 4. The chase problem (case C)

This is the case that decides whether the product works. The naive fix -- "park
it in a corner" -- fails because the corner is where the *next* button is.

Rules, in priority order:

. *Never cover the focused element.* If the float would overlap the element with
   focus, or the caret's line, move it by the smallest displacement that clears
   it.
. *Prefer the nearest free edge*, not a fixed corner. Distance is measured from
   the current position, so successive nudges do not thrash across the screen.
. *Respect exclusion zones.* A site can declare regions that must never be
   covered (for example a review comment box or a CI status panel). Declared
   zones beat the user's preferred corner.
. *Remember where you were and go back.* After a scroll-settle or a dismiss, the
   float returns to its resting geometry, not to wherever it was nudged.
. *Do not chase a moving target.* While the page is scrolling, displacement is
   suspended and `ghosted` takes over instead; a window that jitters during
   scroll is worse than one you cannot see.

An optional later refinement: *learned preference per origin*. If the float is
moved leftward on a given host five times, prefer the left edge there. Learning
is local, explainable, and resettable -- never a hidden model.

== 5. Settings

Stored per manager (userscript) or per profile (app). Every key has a default
that makes the product work with no configuration at all.

[cols="2,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Key | Default | Meaning

| `enabled` | true | Master switch; also reachable from the toolbar
| `geometry` | 480x270, 24 px margin | Resting size and edge inset
| `corner` | `bottom-right` | Preferred resting corner, displaced as needed
| `nudge.enabled` | true | Slide clear of pointer and caret
| `nudge.clearance` | 24 px | Hysteresis distance
| `chip.size` | 48 px | Chipped tab size
| `chip.restore` | `pointer-out` | `pointer-out` \| `delay` \| `click`
| `chip.delayMs` | 1200 | Used when `restore = delay`
| `fling.timerMs` | 0 | 0 means "stay until clicked"
| `banish.timerMs` | 30000 | 0 means "until clicked"
| `ghost.opacity` | 0.15 | Floor opacity while ghosted
| `ghost.mode` | `proximity` | `always` \| `proximity` \| `off`
| `ghost.clickthrough` | true | Route clicks through while ghosted
| `ghost.release` | `hold-alt` | What brings the float back under the pointer
| `motion.reduced` | `system` | `system` \| `always` \| `never`
| `exclusions` | empty | Per-origin regions that must not be covered
| `source` | `pick` | What the float shows: `video` \| `element` \| `panel`
|===

== 6. Accessibility parity

|===
| Behaviour | Pointer | Keyboard | Assistive tech

| On / off | toolbar button | hotkey | menu command with a checked state
| Chip | hover | hotkey toggle | announced "chipped, press again to restore"
| Fling | flick | hotkey + arrow | announced target region
| Banish | flick off edge | hotkey | announced "banished, 30 s"
| Ghost | proximity or menu | hotkey cycle | announced opacity level
| Click-through | automatic | hold modifier | announced "clicks pass through"
| Grabbing drag | drag | arrow-key move, 8 px per press, 64 px with shift | coordinates announced on settle
|===

Rule of thumb: if a state can be entered but not left with the keyboard, the
design is wrong, not the user.

== 7. Edge cases that decide quality

* *Fullscreen video.* The float must not trap focus, and must yield when the page
  enters fullscreen.
* *Cross-origin iframes.* A userscript cannot reach into one. The float can still
  host the iframe element itself; it cannot host a video inside it.
* *DRM content.* Cannot be re-hosted in another element or canvas; only the
  browser's own PiP can show it. This is a hard limit, not a bug.
* *Multi-monitor.* Fling across displays needs the app; a userscript is bounded
  by the viewport of one page.
* *Tab switch.* An in-page float belongs to its page. It vanishes with the tab --
  which is correct behaviour for a *page* float, and the reason the app exists.
* *Printing, zoom, and OS scaling.* Geometry in CSS pixels; recompute on
  `resize`, `orientationchange` and press of zoom.
* *Sites that move their own controls.* Re-evaluate exclusions on mutation, but
  rate-limit to once per frame -- a MutationObserver that fights the page is a
  hang.
* *Two floats.* Out of scope for v1; define it before someone does it twice.

== 8. Mechanisms: what each vehicle can actually do

This section exists because most of the desired behaviours are impossible in the
place people expect them.

[cols="2,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Vehicle | Can | Cannot

| Browser's native PiP window
| Show video, stay above other apps, be moved by the *user*
| Be moved, resized, faded or made click-through by script. It is browser and
  OS property, outside the DOM. No amount of userscript changes that.

| Document Picture-in-Picture (Chrome/Edge 116+, Firefox 151+)
| Carry arbitrary page DOM into a real always-on-top window, with your own
  controls inside it
| Choose its position on screen; fade it; make it click-through; work in Safari
  or on mobile

| In-page float (userscript)
| Position, transform, opacity, click-through, hover, drag, flick -- complete
  control over its own pixels, on any site, with no install
| Leave the page: it dies with the tab and cannot cover other applications

| App window (WebView2 and a native layer)
| True always-on-top across applications, per-window alpha, click-through,
  cross-display fling, survive tab and browser
| Nothing on mobile (see below), and it costs a signed binary

| Android activity PiP
| Float *our own* activity
| Float another app's window -- the OS does not permit it
|===

Consequence: the choreography in §2 lives in the in-page float and in the app
window. Everywhere else it degrades, and §3 of the
link:../TECHNICAL-CONFORMANCE.adoc#_degradation_ladder[conformance document] says
how far.

== 9. Ideas not yet in scope, but worth recording

* *Peek, don't toggle.* Holding a modifier to see through the float is better
  than a persistent setting, because the need is momentary. Cheap to build once
  click-through exists.
* *Dock to the scrollbar.* When scrolling, the least-valuable 20 px of the page
  is the gutter; a float that tucks into it while scrolling never covers content.
* *Follow the caret's line, not the page.* Long documents: the obstruction risk
  is where you are typing, which moves predictably. Track the caret, not the
  page.
* *Collision reporting, local.* Let the user mark "this was in the way" with one
  key, storing an exclusion for that origin. Evidence-driven defaults instead of
  guesswork.
* *A sliver that tells you it is still there.* For audio-only and banished
  states, an edge sliver plus a level meter: visible evidence that something is
  playing, without a window.
* *Choreography as a shared library.* If the motion language (flick, settle,
  dodge, chip) is worth reusing elsewhere in the estate, it should become a
  small, tested motion library rather than bespoke code in this project. Nothing
  in this repository currently claims that; see
  link:IMPLEMENTATION-LANGUAGE.adoc[the language analysis] for what does exist.

== 10. Open questions

. Does the float survive a tab switch as a *miniature* in the toolbar, or die
  with the tab?
. When two behaviours fire at once (pointer near and timer expiring), which wins?
  The rules above say pointer, and say so because the pointer is the thing with
  intent behind it.
. Is "banish off-screen" allowed to be invisible to a screen-sharing session, or
  must it stay in the captured region for honesty?
. One float or several? v1 says one, and the settings schema should not pretend
  otherwise.
